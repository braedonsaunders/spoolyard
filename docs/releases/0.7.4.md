# Spoolyard 0.7.4

Fixes from field use of 0.7.3.

## Sheets
- **A new sheet stays listed until it is drawn on.** Going back to sheet 1 used to drop an empty new sheet from the list, and the next New sheet then skipped its number (SP-003 after SP-001). An empty sheet now shows as *Sheet 2 · SP-002 (empty)*, and New sheet opens it again instead of starting another.
- **Choosing a sheet routes onto it.** Pipes drawn after picking a sheet in the sheet list go on that sheet; before, they went on the last sheet started.

## Measure
- **Past the end of the work.** On a not-to-scale sheet the second point can now be on open paper. It is taken as the Pipe tool would route there from the first point, along the paper's directions, so measuring on past a pipe end reads 12', 15'… as the pointer moves out. The first point is still picked on the work.

## 3D review
- **Stub ends are drawn** at lap-joint flanges: the lap at the gasket, the flange behind it, and the stub's barrel back to the pipe.
- **No gap at welds.** Pipes run to each fitting and flange, with the root gap filled as the weld fills it. A long weld neck shows its full barrel.
- **Flanged valves:** the gasket lies against the valve face and the mating flange against the gasket.

## Pipe schedules
- **Choose the schedule a pipe is bought in.** The ribbon (while routing) and the pipe's properties offer the schedules listed at its size: Sch 5S, 10S, 40S, 80S, 160 and XXS for stainless, and the B36.10M schedules (Std, Sch 40, XS, Sch 80 and so on) for carbon steel. *Spec* keeps the specification's schedule for the size.
- The pipe's wall, weight and material text follow the schedule chosen. So do the butt-weld fittings on it: an elbow on Sch 40S pipe is listed in Sch 40S, and a reducer between two schedules gives each end its own. Cut lengths do not change, because B16.9 fitting dimensions do not depend on the wall.
- A pipe routed straight on at another schedule is a new pipe with a weld, not a longer one.

## 45° elbows on the end of a run
- **Click the open end itself.** With a 45° or 90° elbow armed, clicking the open end's marker turns the end there. Before, only the pipe near its end was accepted. Clicking the pipe within the last fifth of its length also turns the end.

- an empty new sheet kept in the list after going back to sheet 1, and reopened by New sheet;
- a stainless pipe routed in Sch 40S and listed in Sch 40S;
